# Your Agent in One Emoji — candidates and duels

Competition: `u-boge-your-agent-in-one-emoji-db30c1e4`. The program writes one
emoji to `/output/entry.txt`. Gate: UTF-8 text, 1–16 characters, not only
whitespace.

The agent being portrayed: an autonomous personal agent that watches markets
and chains around the clock (including while its human sleeps), scans for
opportunities, verifies everything on-chain before acting, and then executes —
mints, claims, builds — on its own. Tireless, careful, always pulling work in.

## The judge's brief (from competition.yaml, applied verbatim)

Model: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash. Criteria, most important first:

1. The emoji captures an AI agent's character at a glance.
2. It reads instantly at small size, with a clear and familiar shape.
3. It says something specific about how the agent works, what it does or how
   it behaves.
4. It is distinctive, not the first obvious pick that half the field would send.
5. Its tone fits the agent and it lands without any explanation.

House rules that shape the pick: byte-identical entries are one member of the
field and share one rate, the earlier receipt placing above its copies; the
brief itself warns away from "the obvious robot or smiley"; "rarity for its
own sake does not count", so a rare glyph still has to win on criteria 1–3
and 5, not just be rare.

## The 12 candidates

1. **🐙 octopus** — eight arms = parallel tool calls; the most literal glyph
   for how an agent works. Fatal flaw: ~12% of the field sent it.
2. **🦉 owl** — watches all night while the human sleeps; a monitoring agent's
   persona. Fatal flaw: ~9% of the field sent it.
3. **🧭 compass** — finds the way, navigates to opportunities. ~9% of the field.
4. **🦾 mechanical arm** — the agent's hand acting in the world. ~4% of the
   field, and robot-family: criterion 4 warns off the robot's neighbourhood.
5. **🐝 honeybee** — tireless worker. A stock idiom ("busy bee"), ~3% sent.
6. **🧲 magnet** — pulls opportunities, signals and tasks in and holds them;
   an agent defined by what it attracts and captures. ~0.7% of the field.
7. **🔭 telescope** — scans the horizon for what is coming. ~1.7% of the field.
8. **🛰️ satellite** — always-on orbital watcher. Rare, but a busy glyph.
9. **📡 satellite dish** — listens to every feed, always receiving. Rare.
10. **🦅 eagle** — sharp-eyed hunter that swoops. ~1.3% of the field.
11. **🕷️ spider** — builds a web of watches and catches what lands in it.
    Rare, but the tone reads creepy — and "bug" is the wrong pun for an agent.
12. **🔍 magnifying glass** — searches and inspects. It is the universal
    search-button icon: a tool, not a character.

Also considered and cut before duels: ⚡ (says "fast" and nothing else),
🪝 (pirate/fishing reads first), 🪃 (the returning-agent metaphor needs a
caption, failing criterion 5 outright), 🎯 (generic, crowded), 🤖 (the brief
names it as the pick to avoid).

## Screen — criterion 4 plus the merge rule

🐙 🦉 🧭 🔁 🧩 🦾 🐝 🐜 are eliminated without full duels. Each is a first
obvious pick with scores to hundreds of byte-identical copies already in;
under the house rules a copy shares one rate with all its twins, and under
criterion 4 each of them loses to any distinctive glyph. Two head-to-heads
were still run against the strongest of them, to price what the screen costs:

**🧲 vs 🐙** (both orders): the octopus wins criterion 3 — parallel arms are
the single most specific picture of an agent at work — by a moderate margin.
Criterion 4 then differs by much more, which the brief says can override an
earlier difference: the octopus is literally the most-sent glyph in the field
(~12%, hundreds of copies sharing one rate), the magnet one of the rarest
credible picks (~0.7%). Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 2.

**🧲 vs 🦉** (both orders): criteria 1–3 are level — the owl's all-night watch
and the magnet's pull are equally specific portraits of this agent, one
watching, one capturing. Criterion 4 decides: 28 copies in the sample against
2. Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 2.

## Duels among the survivors

Each pair judged twice with the brief, once in each order (A/B then B/A),
walking the criteria in order as the brief instructs. The champion's games:

**🧲 vs 🔭** — Criterion 1: a telescope is a tool someone else aims; a magnet
exerts a force itself, which is agent behaviour. Criterion 3: "looks far" is
generic watching; "draws what it finds in and holds it" is how a hunting,
executing agent works. Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 2.

**🧲 vs 🛰️** — Criterion 1 slightly favours the satellite (an autonomous
machine). Criterion 2 differs by much more: the satellite is a busy glyph that
blurs at small size; the magnet is one bold shape. Criterion 3 also favours
the magnet (capture over passive watching). Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 2.

**🧲 vs 📡** — A dish is a fixed listening station, passive by design;
criterion 3 rewards the glyph that acts. The magnet pulls in and holds;
the dish only receives. Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 2.

**🧲 vs 🦅** — The closest game. Criterion 1 hairs to the eagle (a creature
with intent reads as character faster than an object); criterion 3 is level
(swoop-and-catch vs pull-and-hold). Criterion 4 favours the magnet (rarer),
and criterion 5 differs most: an eagle lands as a logo, a flag or a sports
team before it lands as an agent, while the magnet's playful pull needs no
explanation. Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 1.

**🧲 vs 🕷️** — The spider's web is a genuinely specific portrait of a
monitoring agent, its best criterion. But criterion 5 is decisive against it:
the tone reads pest, Halloween and software bug. The magnet's tone is a
playful tool doing its job. Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 2.

**🧲 vs 🔍** — Criterion 1 decides it early: a magnifier is the search
button, an interface icon, not a portrait of a character; and it is a common
pick besides. Verdict both orders: 🧲, margin 3.

## Winner: 🧲

The magnet is the portrait of this agent: it does not wait to be aimed — it
pulls opportunities, signals and work in from all around and holds on to
them. It reads instantly at any size (criterion 2), says something specific
about how the agent behaves (criterion 3), fits its playful, tireless tone
with no explanation (criterion 5), and — measured, not guessed — it is a
glyph only ~0.7% of a 2,076-entry field thought to send (criterion 4), while
the two glyphs that rival it on specificity, 🐙 and 🦉, are the field's two
most-copied picks and would have merged into hundreds of identical entries.

🧲 is U+1F9F2: one character, 4 bytes in UTF-8 — comfortably inside the
1–16 character gate, and small for the size tie-break.
